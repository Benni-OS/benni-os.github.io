Autonomous Cinema Engineering: Deterministic 60 FPS Video Synthesis from AST State Graphs Without Pixel Hallucination

Why generative diffusion models fail for exact enterprise product demos, and how Benni Video Engine compiles mathematical motion and acoustic foley directly from code.

1. The Generative Diffusion Fallacy in Enterprise Media

While diffusion models like Sora and Runway Gen-3 excel at artistic fantasy scenes, they fail catastrophically for enterprise software walkthroughs, architectural explainers, and product launch films.

Their probabilistic nature leads to:

  • Pixel Hallucination & Typography Drift: Code blocks warp into illegible glyphs, button radii mutate between frames, and logos drift across perspective planes.
  • Zero Temporal Editability: Changing a button color at second 14 requires re-generating the entire video from scratch.
  • Excessive Cost & Latency: Generating a single 60-second clip costs $4 to $12 in cloud GPU cycles with minutes of latency.

2. The Code-First Autonomous Cinema Paradigm

Benni Video Engine OS (Skills 65, 66, and 67) compiles video deterministically from Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) state graphs into mathematically exact 60/120 FPS animations without pixel hallucination:

  • One Shape Never Cut (Skill 65): Continuous homotopy deformation between visual scenes without jarring jump cuts.
  • Optical Depth-of-Field & Bokeh Shaders (Skill 66): 5-plane Circle of Confusion blur with true painter's algorithm Z-sorting and linear shutter motion blur (γ = 2.2).
  • Speed-Ramp Dynamics (Skill 67): Smooth transitions from 4.2x whip-cuts to 0.14x bullet-time slow-motion using closed-form 2nd-order harmonic spring ODEs with conservative volume preservation (det(J) = 1).
  • Procedural Acoustic Foley: 44.1kHz synthetic mechanical keystrokes, pneumatic whooshes, and sub-bass impacts locked to a 120 BPM tempo grid.
